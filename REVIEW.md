## Detailed Defects and Rectification Plan

**1. Kitchen Otta (Under-Counter) Finishing:** In the attached photo of the kitchen area, the plaster finish under the counter (above the plinth) is uneven and the skirting is missing (exposing raw mortar or wall). For interior finishes, any uneven or unfinished surfaces must be addressed. Leaving the underside unfinished is unacceptable because gaps collect dirt and moisture, detracting from aesthetics and potentially allowing seepage or pests.  

- *Rectification Steps:* Remove any loose plaster or mortar. Apply a new cement mortar (1:3 cement:sand) base layer to level the surface flush with the cabinet bottom. Once dried, apply a smooth plaster skim coat. Install a continuous skirting strip (tile or PVC matching the kitchen finish) along the base. Ensure edges are sealed, then repaint or touch up to match the cabinet color.  
- *Inspection/Test:* Verify the plaster surface is even (no bulges or recesses) and painted uniformly. The skirting must be fully bonded and level; use a straightedge to confirm there is no visible gap greater than ~2 mm between the skirting and floor.  
- *Priority/Timeline:* **Medium priority.** Complete within 1 week to allow final painting.  

**2. Main Door Frame and Finish:** Photos of the main entrance show the door frame set out of plumb – it does not line up with the surrounding wall – and the plaster/paint around the frame is rough and patchy. Slight door misalignment can be tolerated if the door closes and latches correctly, but here the frame is visibly uneven. This is unacceptable because it can impair the door’s operation, allow drafts or water leaks, and is unsightly for an entry point.  

- *Rectification Steps:* Loosen the frame fixings and realign the door frame so it is plumb (use a level to achieve uniform reveal gap ~2–3 mm). Pack any gaps behind the frame with cement mortar or appropriate filler. Re-plaster the gap flush with the wall. Once set, repaint the frame and surrounding wall area to match existing finish. Also address any white plaster patches by sanding and repainting.  
- *Inspection/Test:* Check that the door swings freely and locks securely. Measure the gap around the frame – it should be even on all sides (within 2–3 mm tolerance) and the frame should be square. The painted finish should be smooth and uniform with no patchiness.  
- *Priority/Timeline:* **High priority.** This is a critical entry point; fix within 3 days.  

**3. Main Door Threshold Skirting:** The skirting or tile strip at the bottom of the main door threshold is missing (see photo). This leaves a gap below the door. An unfinished threshold is unacceptable because it can allow dirt and moisture ingress and fails handover standards.  

- *Rectification Steps:* Cut and install a matching threshold piece (tile or skirting) to span the gap under the door. Use an appropriate adhesive and grout to secure it. Ensure the new piece is level with the floor and the door frame base.  
- *Inspection/Test:* The threshold skirting must form a continuous strip across the doorway with no visible openings. Joint gaps should be minimal (≤2 mm) and grouted. Confirm the threshold is flush and there is no uneven step.  
- *Priority/Timeline:* **Medium priority.** Coordinate this with the door fix above; complete within 1 week.  

**4. Balcony Sliding Door Frame and Skirting:** Images of the balcony door area show unfinished plaster and missing skirting around the sliding door frame (top and sides). Incomplete finishing here can permit water penetration behind the walls and looks unfinished. Proper sealing of exterior doors is required to prevent leaks.  

- *Rectification Steps:* Fill gaps in the plaster around the sliding-door frame with cement mortar and smooth it flush. Install any missing skirting tiles or strips along the bottom of the door recess so the edge is protected. Once set, apply a waterproof sealant/paint coat as needed. Touch-up paint to match existing surfaces.  
- *Inspection/Test:* Ensure the plaster joints around the frame are smooth and sealed (use a straightedge to confirm no unevenness >3 mm). The skirting at the base of the door should align with adjacent floor tiles without gaps. Verify the door still operates smoothly after work.  
- *Priority/Timeline:* **Medium priority.** Address within 1 week to finalize weatherproofing.  

**5. Broken Balcony Tile:** The attached photo shows a cracked/broken floor tile on the balcony. This is unacceptable as it poses a trip hazard and may allow water ingress under the tile layer, damaging the substrate.  

- *Rectification Steps:* Remove the broken tile and clear old adhesive. Inspect and repair the floor screed if needed. Install a new matching tile using tile adhesive, press into place, and grout the joints to match the pattern. Seal edges as per spec.  
- *Inspection/Test:* The replacement tile must be level and flush with the surrounding tiles. Grout joints should be even (about 2–3 mm) and fully filled. After curing, gently tap the tile – it should sound solid (no hollow sound indicating poor adhesion). Confirm there are no cracks.  
- *Priority/Timeline:* **High priority.** Repair immediately (within 3 days) for safety and finish integrity.  

**6. Balcony Corner Drain (Sump) Cover:** The photo shows the balcony floor drain/sump area open (no cover/grating). Leaving this gap is unsafe, allows debris entry, and may undermine the waterproofing over time. Proper drainage detailing is required to prevent pooling or erosion.  

- *Rectification Steps:* Install a suitable drain cover or metal grating over the opening (sized for the existing hole). Secure it into the mortar so it’s flush with the tile finish. Ensure a waterproof seal around its edges.  
- *Inspection/Test:* Perform a water test – pour water around the drain to verify it flows smoothly into the drain without backing up. Confirm the cover is firmly fixed (no movement) and sits level with the surrounding floor. There should be no pooling.  
- *Priority/Timeline:* **Medium priority.** Complete within 1 week as part of final waterproofing.  

Each item above should be corrected using standard materials and workmanship: use proper cement/sand mixes, high-quality tile adhesive and grout, and skilled plaster finishing. After repairs, all visible finishes (plaster, paint, tiles) should meet standard tolerances (plaster levelness within ~2 mm, grout joints uniform, door alignment per manufacturer specs, etc.).
